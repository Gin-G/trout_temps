Why 65°F? Thresholds vary by species
The angling threshold is the temperature at which catch-and-release mortality starts climbing sharply with every further degree. These come from Keep Fish Wet’s review of 24 mortality studies and 9 sublethal-impact studies, set to hold temperature-related mortality under 5–10%.
SpeciesAngling threshold
Bull trout54°F · 12°C
Rainbow, steelhead, cutthroat, brook61°F · 16°C
Brown trout66°F · 19°C
The line this page draws65°F · 18°C

A USGS gage reports water temperature, not what swims past it, so every gage here is scored against one 65°F line. That sits about where brown trout are, and roughly 4°F above rainbow, cutthroat and brook trout — on those waters, read the caution band as the real limit. The older 68°F rule of thumb is higher than the review supports for any of these species. Source: Keep Fish Wet, Fishing for Trout in Warming Waters ↗

Trout water temperature questions

Is it too hot to fish for trout?

Once water passes about 65°F (18°C) it is too hot to fish for trout you intend to release. Warm water holds less dissolved oxygen at the same time a fought fish needs more of it, so trout that swim away can still die hours later. Below 60°F you are in good shape; 60–65°F is the caution band.

What water temperature is too warm for trout?

It depends on the species. Bull trout are done at 54°F, rainbow, steelhead, cutthroat and brook trout at about 61°F, and brown trout at about 66°F. This page scores every gage against a single 65°F line, so on rainbow, cutthroat and brook water treat the caution band as the real limit.

How do I check the water temperature of a river before I fish?

The USGS operates streamgages that report water temperature every 15 to 60 minutes, published free as parameter 00010. This page reads those gages live for 15 states and converts them to °F, so you can check a river's current temperature without a thermometer before you drive out.

Why does warm water kill trout after catch and release?

Fighting a fish burns oxygen and floods its muscles with lactic acid. In cold water a trout clears that and recovers. In warm water there is less dissolved oxygen available to repay the debt, so mortality after release climbs sharply with every degree above the threshold.

When is the best time to fish in hot weather?

Early morning. Water is coldest just after dawn and warmest in late afternoon, so a stream that is borderline at 4pm may be well within safe range at 6am. Tailwaters below bottom-release dams and high-elevation or spring-fed streams also stay colder through a heat wave.

What is hoot owl fishing and when does it apply?

Hoot owl restrictions close fishing from 2pm to midnight when water gets too warm, leaving only the cold morning hours open. States such as Montana impose them during summer heat and low flows. They are set by your state agency, so check current regulations and voluntary closures alongside this page.
